Science behind the Eco-Quest

  • Industrial Outskirts. OCO-2 and OCO-3 retrieve column-averaged atmospheric CO₂ (XCO₂, ppm) from reflected sunlight. Scientists combine concentrations with models to infer sources and sinks. The DYAMOND image below shows modeled CO₂ transport. NASA OCO-2 · NASA SVS DYAMOND
  • Sunlit Meadow. GEDI laser returns reveal the vertical structure of forests. Canopy structure helps researchers study habitat and estimate aboveground biomass.
  • Root Cavern. SMAP uses microwave observations to estimate water content in the top roughly 5 cm of soil. Water availability influences roots and microbial activity.
  • Coral Coastline. SWOT measures the elevation of water surfaces across swaths. Scientists use these data to study oceans, lakes and rivers. The shoreline litter quest is an educational game. NASA SWOT
  • Deep Reef Sanctuary. PACE measures reflected light at many wavelengths. Ocean color helps researchers study phytoplankton. Reef pH in the puzzle is an illustrative game score.

How NASA data becomes music

  1. Choose a NASA frame, a dated map, or a GISTEMP year.
  2. The Jukebox reads frame colors or uses the GISTEMP numerical anomaly.
  3. It maps the input into a fixed musical range, then sends the pitch, tempo, filter, and stereo position to Web Audio.

Open a track and use Hear this moment to trigger a note using the current frame or year. The telemetry beside the spectrum shows the value and the parameters used.

In the Interactive Simulator and Eco-Quest, the sound engine uses the animated canvases, hypothetical environmental state, and quest rewards.

Ocean Acidification & Reef Chemistry

CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻

Excess atmospheric CO₂ dissolves into seawater to form carbonic acid (H₂CO₃), releasing H⁺ ions that lower pH. Open-ocean surface pH averages roughly 8.1 today and varies by region and time.

CaCO₃ (Coral Skeleton) + H⁺ → Ca²⁺ + HCO₃⁻

Higher acidity makes it harder for corals and shellfish to build calcium carbonate skeletons.

Photosynthesis & Carbon Sinks

6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Sunlight → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Plants and phytoplankton use light to produce sugars and oxygen. Some of the carbon taken up by plants is stored in vegetation or soils, while respiration and decomposition release some of it.

Radiative Forcing

ΔF ≈ 5.35 × ln(C / C₀) W/m²

In this approximation, doubling CO₂ adds roughly 3.7 W/m² of radiative forcing. The simulator uses a separate educational warming response.

Key Earth & Climate Milestones

  • 1760 — Industrial Revolution: Coal-powered industry begins raising atmospheric CO₂ from ~280 ppm.
  • 1958 — The Keeling Curve: Charles David Keeling begins precision CO₂ measurements at Mauna Loa (315 ppm).
  • 1970 — Clean Air Act: Landmark policy cuts sulfur dioxide and soot, proving clean air regulations work.
  • 1987 — Montreal Protocol: Global treaty phases out ozone-depleting CFCs so the ozone layer can heal.
  • 2015 — Paris Agreement: Countries agree to hold warming well below 2 °C and pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5 °C above pre-industrial levels.
